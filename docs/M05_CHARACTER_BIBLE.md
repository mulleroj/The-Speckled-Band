# M05.1 — Character Bible

**Status:** proposed design targets; creator approval required before portrait generation.
**Evidence key:** **Source-backed** means described in Conan Doyle's 1892 story. **Adaptation choice** means a consistent visual proposal not fixed by the story. The source does not provide a definitive character sheet or exact measurements.

## Shared portrait rules

Use the approved literary illustration treatment from `M05_VISUAL_BIBLE.md`. Keep expressions legible but restrained. Use neutral, comparable portrait lighting and scale for the four master sheets. Never use a caricature, a modern celebrity likeness, ethnic coding, or an unapproved deerstalker as a shortcut for identity. Keep story-specific variations (wet clothing, veil, fatigue, pose) separate from the canonical base model.

## Sherlock Holmes

| Feature | Profile |
|---|---|
| Age | **Adaptation choice:** approximately 32–38. The 1892 story does not state his age. |
| Face | **Adaptation choice aligned to brief:** long, narrow face; high cheekbones; clean, sharply defined nose and jaw; attentive mouth. Avoid an exaggerated hawk profile. |
| Build | **Adaptation choice aligned to brief:** tall, lean, long-limbed, economical in movement. |
| Hair | **Adaptation choice:** dark brown, neat, naturally waved or brushed back; no theatrical forelock. Exact style is not fixed in this story. |
| Eyes | **Adaptation choice:** grey-brown or cool hazel, observant rather than glowing or unnaturally piercing. |
| Clothing | **Adaptation choice:** well-cut dark Victorian lounge suit or frock coat, high-collared shirt, modest neckwear; adapt layers to scene and weather. No automatic deerstalker indoors. |
| Posture | Upright or relaxed with deliberate stillness; leans in when attending to a detail; gestures are precise, not grand. |
| Emotional range | Reserved, intellectually alert, dryly amused, compassionate in brief practical gestures. Avoid smugness and omniscient certainty. |
| Props | Case-book, papers, magnifier only when text calls for one; no invented signature pipe, violin, or weapon in a scene without textual warrant. |
| Master views | Neutral front, three-quarter, profile, seated conversational pose, standing full figure; expression strip for attentive, analytical, gently reassuring, dryly amused. Include indoor baseline without hat and a separate approved outdoor layer/hat variation. |
| Continuity | Stable lean silhouette, face and hair. Shirt, collar, waistcoat, coat and tie remain identical through continuous Baker Street scenes unless a documented time jump justifies a change. |

## Helen Stoner

| Feature | Profile |
|---|---|
| Age | **Source-backed:** Helen says she and Julia were twins and that Julia was thirty at death two years earlier; Helen is therefore about 32 in the 1883 visit, subject to the story's dates. **Adaptation choice:** portray her as an adult in her early thirties, not as a teenager. |
| Face | **Source-backed:** the account describes a drawn, grey face, weary expression, and restless frightened eyes at the first meeting. **Adaptation choice:** render fatigue with restrained under-eye shadow and pallor, not illness, grotesquerie, or glamour. |
| Build | **Source-backed:** no precise build is stated. **Adaptation choice:** adult, ordinary proportions; do not make her physically frail by default. |
| Hair | **Source-backed:** premature greying is mentioned for Helen and Julia. **Adaptation choice:** dark brown hair with visible early silver strands, consistent with the source. |
| Eyes | **Source-backed:** frightened, restless eyes are described at the first meeting. **Adaptation choice:** natural dark hazel or brown; exact color is unspecified. |
| Clothing | **Source-backed:** at the opening she wears black and a heavy veil; the source mentions a jacket sleeve and glove. **Adaptation choice:** period-appropriate dark travel dress, jacket, glove, veil and modest lace; colors/material may vary only where the adaptation establishes them. |
| Posture | Initially tense and guarded; seated near the window, then able to choose to speak and show her wrist. Her fear must coexist with agency and self-possession. |
| Emotional range | Fear, exhaustion, grief, trust growing through being heard, and quiet resolve. Never helpless, hysterical, seductive, or defined only by victimhood. |
| Props | Veil, glove, return-ticket portion, sleeve with fresh road mud in Chapter I; wrist marks only when the locked scene exposes them and with no graphic detail. |
| Master views | Neutral front and three-quarter; profile with veil; seated interview pose; standing full figure in travel clothes; hands/glove/sleeve reference; expression sheet for wary, fearful, composed, and determined. Make a separate post-interview state only if the script requires it. |
| Continuity | Same adult face, early-thirties age, hair with restrained premature silvering, dress and travel wear across the Baker Street interview. Do not move or enlarge marks, mud, or ticket between views. In the consent scene, her own hand turns; Holmes does not touch her. |

## Dr Grimesby Roylott

| Feature | Profile |
|---|---|
| Age | **Source-backed:** described as an old man in his Baker Street appearance; no exact age is provided. **Adaptation choice:** late fifties to late sixties, visibly older than Holmes and Watson. |
| Face | **Source-backed:** large, deeply lined, sun-weathered face; deep-set eyes and a high, thin nose. **Adaptation choice:** naturalistic planes and lines, never bird, beast, demon, or grotesque exaggeration. |
| Build | **Source-backed:** very tall, broad, and immensely strong; he nearly fills the doorway and bends the poker. |
| Hair | **Source-backed:** the passage does not establish hair color or style. **Adaptation choice:** greying dark hair, brushed back, kept consistent and secondary to his face. |
| Eyes | **Source-backed:** “bile-shot” describes the eyes' appearance in the original literary wording: a bile-like yellow/brownish cast, not simply ordinary bloodshot redness and not a medical diagnosis. **Adaptation choice:** keep the eyes deep-set and naturalistic, with at most a restrained yellow-brown cast in the whites; do not render them as glowing, vividly jaundiced, or uniformly red. |
| Clothing | **Source-backed:** the Baker Street entrance includes a black top hat, long frock coat, high gaiters, and mixed professional/agricultural dress; he carries a hunting crop. **Adaptation choice:** restrained earth-dark palette, plausible wear, no armor-like silhouette. |
| Posture | Broad, imposing stance; abrupt forward movement communicates threat. Keep human anatomy and credible weight. Do not add restraints, weapons, or looming camera distortion. |
| Emotional range | Volatile, proud, threatening, capable of sudden anger. Show menace through action, scale, and expression, not a villain grin or theatrical rage in every frame. |
| Props | Hunting/riding crop at the Baker Street visit; fireplace steel poker during the specified event. The poker's bent/straight states are tracked separately. No unrequested animal or exotic prop in Chapter I–II visuals. |
| Master views | Neutral front, three-quarter and profile; doorway full figure with hat and outdoor coat; hand scale reference; controlled anger, interrogative stare, and neutral pose. Keep prop-free neutral master in addition to costume/prop variants. |
| Continuity | Height and shoulder breadth remain consistent against the doorway and Holmes. The crop is not the poker. Keep hand positions and poker state aligned with the scene chronology. No racialized features or ethnic shorthand. |

## Evidence and approval record

The original story supports the listed specific observations, ages where stated, clothing details where stated, and Roylott's height/strength. Exact face shapes beyond the source description, Holmes/Watson/Helen eye and hair colors, most age choices, body proportions beyond the text, and all polished costume design remain adaptation decisions. Record a creator-approved reference image and version for each character before M05.2 is considered complete. Approval covers likeness continuity, not any future scene composition.

## Explicit adaptation departure: Helen's wrist

In the 1892 original, Holmes himself pushes back the lace and reveals the five marks. The approved Chapter II adaptation changes this beat: Holmes asks permission, Helen chooses to turn her own hand and show her wrist, and Holmes does not touch her. This is an intentional, approved adaptation decision—not a claim about the original passage. Illustrations must follow the adapted action and preserve Helen's agency.
