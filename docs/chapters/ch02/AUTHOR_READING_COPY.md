## S01 — The Hidden Bruises

The fire had burned low. Miss Stoner sat very still, one gloved hand resting on the chair. Holmes watched her with the quiet attention he gave to a difficult detail. As she moved, the lace at her wrist slipped back.

**HOLMES:** Miss Stoner, may I ask you one more question?
**HELEN:** Yes, Mr Holmes.
**HOLMES:** May I see your wrist?

Helen looked at him for a moment. Then she turned her hand. Five dark marks showed against her skin. Holmes did not touch her.

**HOLMES:** Five marks. They look like the marks of a hand.
**HELEN:** My stepfather has a hard grip. Perhaps he does not know his own strength.

She drew her sleeve back into place. Her voice was quiet, but she did not look away.

**HOLMES:** You need not explain more than you wish.
**HELEN:** Perhaps the dates matter. Julia was to marry shortly before she died. I am to marry this spring.

Holmes let the silence stand for a few seconds. Then he turned back to the matter of the house.

**WATSON — choose a response:**

Helen answers the first: “Thank you, Dr Watson. It is sore, but I can manage. I want you to understand why I am afraid.” She answers the second: “Thank you. I do not know what my stepfather will do when he returns.”

**HOLMES:** Could we see the rooms at Stoke Moran today, while Dr Roylott is away?
**HELEN:** He said he had business in town. He should be away most of the day. I can meet you at home.
**HOLMES:** Then Watson and I shall come this afternoon.

As Helen gathered her veil, I opened my notebook. I paused over the empty page, searching for the right words.

**WATSON — observation challenge:** Which note records only what you can see?

- “Helen says her stepfather has a hard grip.”

**Feedback after the attempt:** A: “That is important. Mark it as Helen's report.” B: “Yes. This records what you can see.” C: “We can see marks on Helen's wrist. They do not tell us how Julia died.”

Helen covered her face with her veil when she was ready to go. The door closed behind her. Holmes and I were left with a promise to meet—and a house we had not yet seen.

## S02 — A Difficult Puzzle

For a little while Holmes said nothing. He stood by the window, looking down at the street. I thought of the low whistle and the strange words Helen had repeated. The marks on her wrist gave me another question, but not an answer.

**HOLMES:** What do you make of Helen's story, Watson?
**WATSON:** Julia heard a low whistle. Before she died, she spoke of a speckled band. Helen does not know what either means.

**WATSON — choose the thought that stays closest to the facts:**

- “The whistle caused Julia's death.”
- “Julia's last words tell us exactly what the speckled band was.”
- “The whistle and her last words may be connected, but we need more facts.”

**Feedback after the attempt:** “That is possible, but the story has not shown a cause.” / “Those words are a clue to follow, not an explanation yet.” / “A careful possibility. We must see the place before we can judge it.”

**HOLMES:** And what of the marks we have just seen?
**WATSON:** They show that force was used on Helen's wrist. They do not show what happened in Julia's room.
**HOLMES:** A cautious answer, Watson. Let us see whether the house is more helpful than its owner.

Holmes turned from the window. A faint smile crossed his face, then was gone.

**HOLMES:** Helen says Roylott is in town today. We should make use of the time.

## S03 — The Unwelcome Visitor

The door flew open. A huge man filled the doorway, a riding crop swinging from one hand. He looked first at Holmes, then at me. The room seemed smaller with him in it.

**ROYLOTT:** Which of you is Holmes?
**HOLMES:** I am. And you are?
**ROYLOTT:** Dr Grimesby Roylott. My stepdaughter has been here. I followed her. What has she told you?

Holmes did not rise. His eyes moved once to Roylott's hands and then back to his face.

**HOLMES:** It is a little cold for April, is it not?
**ROYLOTT:** Do not mock me. Keep out of my affairs. I am a dangerous man to cross.

He stepped forward and seized the steel poker by the fire.

**WATSON — choose a response:**

- Say nothing. Watch his hands and listen.
- “You have made your warning clear, Dr Roylott.”

If Watson names the threat, Holmes signals him to stay back. Either response continues to the same event.

**ROYLOTT:** See what I can do! Keep out of my grip!

He bent the iron with both hands and flung it down. Then he turned and strode from the room. The door shook after him.

## S04 — The Bent Poker

The silence after his departure felt almost as heavy as his voice. Holmes picked up the poker and worked it straight. Metal scraped against metal. I watched his hands, then looked at the door.

**WATSON — evidence challenge:** What does the poker incident support?

- “A strong man must have caused Julia's death.”
- “Roylott bent the poker here. It shows his strength, not how Julia died.”
- “The poker shows what happened in Julia's room.”

**Feedback after the attempt:** A: “That is more than this event tells us.” B: “Yes. You have kept the event separate from the death.” C: “The poker was here in Baker Street. We have not connected it to Julia's room.”

**HOLMES:** A powerful grip, Watson. It is a warning, and a rude one.
**HOLMES:** No. We saw what he did here. We did not see what happened to Julia.
**HOLMES:** A neat demonstration, if one has no better argument.
**WATSON:** He followed Helen here. Should she go back alone?
**HOLMES:** I hope she will not suffer for trusting us. Let us not waste the time she has given us.

Holmes asked me to wait while he checked the legal record. I heard the clock mark the minutes after the door had closed behind him.

## S05 — The Price of Marriage

When Holmes returned, he carried a sheet of blue paper covered with figures. The wait had been long, but his eyes were bright with the pleasure of a question answered.

**HOLMES:** I have seen the will of Helen's mother. I checked the present value of the investments.
**WATSON:** What did you find?
**HOLMES:** The income is now about seven hundred and fifty pounds a year. If a daughter marries, she may receive two hundred and fifty pounds a year.

**WATSON — deduction challenge:** Julia was to marry before she died. Helen plans to marry this spring. What do these figures support?

- “The figures show that marriage would make no difference to Roylott.”
- “The will proves Roylott killed Julia.”
- “A daughter's marriage could reduce the income Roylott controls. This gives him a possible motive, not proof.”

**Feedback after the attempt:** A: “Look at what the will allows a daughter to receive.” B: “A motive can guide an investigation, but it cannot prove what happened.” C: “Yes. The figures point to a reason to investigate, not a verdict.”

**HOLMES:** A reason to fear a wedding, perhaps. But a reason is not proof of a crime.
**WATSON:** The figures explain why he might oppose a marriage. They do not tell us how Julia died.
**HOLMES:** Precisely. We must see the house.

## S06 — The Journey Begins

Holmes folded the paper and put it away. The morning's questions had taken shape, but none had yet become an answer. Outside, the street below was busy with carts and voices.

**HOLMES:** We have Helen's account, Roylott's warning, and the figures from the will. We still do not know what caused Julia's death.
**WATSON:** Then we must look at the rooms before we decide what the facts mean.
**HOLMES:** Just so. Waterloo, Watson. We have no time to lose.
