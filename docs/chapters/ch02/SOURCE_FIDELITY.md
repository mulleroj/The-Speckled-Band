# Chapter II — Source fidelity audit

**Primary source:** Arthur Conan Doyle, “The Adventure of the Speckled Band” (1892), [Wikisource transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Sherlock_Holmes_(1892,_US)/The_Adventure_of_the_Speckled_Band). Scene references below follow the source sequence and paragraph content; the adaptation uses newly written B1 dialogue and does not transcribe a modern screen adaptation.

## Continuity decision

Current `src/content/ch01.js` closing: Helen mentions Julia's last words; Holmes says they must examine Stoke Moran and not pretend to know the answer; “The first case file is open. The mystery has only begun.” It does **not** say Helen has left. Therefore the approved S01 can continue immediately with Holmes noticing her wrist before she leaves, without revising Chapter I runtime. The current Chapter I's intent to investigate is compatible with S01 later arranging the visit that afternoon.

This is a staging compression: in Doyle's order, Holmes discovers the wrist marks before proposing the visit that day. Chapter I has already voiced a general wish to examine Stoke Moran, so S01 must not repeat it as a new revelation; it turns that intention into an agreed appointment. If the author later considers even this reordering unacceptable, stop and request separate approval before changing Chapter I.

## Facts deliberately not introduced in this Chapter II script

- Cause or mechanism of Julia's death; the eventual canonical solution.
- A match, metallic sound, barred window, sealed room, chimney details, layout, or animal. Current Chapter I adaptation does not give these facts to the player, and Chapter II is not the house inspection.
- Roma people as suspects, criminal associates, or evidence. The original wording is historically prejudiced and not a reliable investigative claim.
- Modern medical diagnosis or a claim that the wrist marks establish a date or exact sequence.
- A claim that Holmes or Watson saw Helen's will. Holmes reports his own consultation and calculation.
