# M05.1 — Location Bible

**Status:** design brief only. Location masters require creator approval.
**Source rule:** distinguish rooms and journeys explicitly established in the 1892 story from proposed camera views and material treatments. Chapter III onward remains unimplemented; this document must not be treated as a scene script.

## 221B Baker Street — exterior

- **Story basis:** Holmes and Watson share rooms in Baker Street; the case opens early in April, 1883. The story does not give a definitive facade design or street number exterior view in this scene.
- **Atmosphere:** waking London, cool dawn, quiet city activity; literary and lived-in, not fog-bound by default.
- **Light:** pale overcast morning or restrained street gaslight where appropriate. Keep the house number readable only if the scene needs it; do not invent signage that implies an exact documented facade.
- **Materials:** plausible late-Victorian London brick, stone lintel, painted door and sash windows. Treat as an adaptation design, not an archival reconstruction.
- **Perspective:** street-level view with a modest oblique angle that establishes an urban home without grand establishing-shot spectacle.
- **Orientation anchors:** entrance door, repeated window rhythm, narrow street frontage. Lock chosen facade, window count and neighboring building relationships for all exterior returns.

## 221B Baker Street — shared sitting room

- **Story basis:** a shared sitting room, fireplace, chairs, window, desk and Holmes's case-book are described or used. Helen sits by the window; Holmes attends to her; Roylott later enters through the door. Details in the locked adaptations add stable staging.
- **Atmosphere:** intellectually busy but orderly, intimate, warm against London morning. Preserve space for a serious conversation.
- **Light:** cool daylight at the window balanced by low firelight. The fire can be low in Chapter II; do not shift the source direction between continuous scenes.
- **Materials:** dark timber, subdued patterned upholstery, plaster, brass or iron fireplace fittings, paper and wool. Avoid a cluttered Victorian prop collection.
- **Perspective:** establish one room master, then a small set of repeatable camera positions: door-facing, window-facing, fireplace/desk angle, and seated two-shot. Keep doorway, window, hearth, chairs and desk spatially coherent.
- **Orientation anchors:** window at the same wall; fireplace and poker; entry door; Holmes's desk/case-book; Helen's chair; Watson's writing position. Annotate each in a top-down continuity plan before image generation.
- **UI fit:** keep the lower title area and top chapter/clock overlays low-detail in the scene-stage crop. Do not put a clue behind a heading.

## Waterloo Station and train

- **Story basis:** Holmes and Watson take a cab to Waterloo and board a train toward Leatherhead at the end of Chapter II. The current locked chapter ends as the train departs; it does not show arrival or travel to Stoke Moran.
- **Atmosphere:** purposeful public movement, controlled bustle, a transition from city investigation to a journey. No action-movie urgency.
- **Light:** diffuse daylight for the Chapter II departure, filtered through station roof and steam only as historically appropriate. Keep smoke subtle and readable.
- **Materials:** period railway ironwork, brick, timber, glass, carriage paint, brass fittings and printed paper. Avoid contemporary platform markings, electric displays, modern signage, or anachronistic uniforms.
- **Perspective:** platform-height medium-wide composition; distinguish platform edge, carriage doorway and train direction. Avoid confusing a departure with arrival.
- **Orientation anchors:** carriage side/door, platform canopy, rails and direction of travel. The scene's focal action is boarding/departure; no Stoke Moran manor, countryside reveal, or future clue belongs in this frame.

## Stoke Moran — exterior

- **Story basis:** an old ancestral manor house in Surrey, financially diminished. The original describes a high central portion with two curved wings projecting on either side. One wing has broken windows boarded with wood and a partly collapsed roof; the central portion is also in poor repair. The right-hand block is comparatively modern and is where the family resides. Preserve these as distinct source descriptions; do not invent extra wings or silently relocate the occupied rooms. Animals mentioned in the story must not be used as decorative threat shorthand or shown before an approved scene requires them.
- **Atmosphere:** aged, inhabited, weathered and socially isolated; quiet unease rather than supernatural ruin. It is a lived-in country house, not a haunted castle.
- **Light:** match the chapter's established weather and time. Daylight should reveal architectural relationships; night scenes may preserve enough visibility to orient doors and windows.
- **Materials:** old stone and brick, weathered timber, plaster, glass, iron fittings, overgrown but plausible grounds. Avoid generic gothic spikes, impossible scale, and decay inconsistent with occupation.
- **Perspective:** one exterior master shows central block and inhabited wing clearly enough to support later interior geography. Use restrained distance; do not turn the house into a misleading maze.
- **Orientation anchors:** central portion, the two curved wings, the ruined wing, the comparatively modern right-hand block, lawn, paths and windows. Lock this three-part facade geometry and the approach route before making interiors. The text establishes these relationships but does not provide measured architectural plans; exact lengths, angles and connection details remain design decisions.

## Stoke Moran — interior and investigation corridor

- **Story basis:** only one wing is inhabited; bedrooms occupy the ground floor in that wing, while sitting rooms are in the central portion. Along the inhabited wing, viewed from the lawn/facade as Holmes identifies the windows, the order from the outer end toward the main building is: Helen's former bedroom, Julia's bedroom in the middle, then Dr Roylott's room nearest the main building. Helen now sleeps in Julia's former middle room while repairs are underway in her own room. All three doors open from the corridor running along the other side of this narrow wing; the rooms' windows face the lawn. Chapter III must confirm only what its approved scene allows Watson to see.
- **Atmosphere:** practical domestic rooms with age, repair and uneven upkeep. Unease comes from distance, silence and human use, not from spooky effects.
- **Light:** establish corridor lamp positions and window daylight consistently. In night scenes, retain the actual light sources and enough visibility for fair observation; do not conceal required facts in pure black.
- **Materials:** old plaster, timber doors and floors, iron hardware, curtain fabric, glass, and repair materials. Match aging and wear across adjoining spaces.
- **Perspective:** establish a simple floor plan before illustration: central block, inhabited wing, corridor, each room door, each lawn-facing window, and the relation of the corridor to the grounds. Use repeatable eye-level views and avoid impossible sightlines.
- **Orientation anchors:** keep the lawn-facing window order (Helen's former room → Julia's middle room, now Helen's → Roylott's room nearest the main building) aligned with the opposite-side corridor doors; include the corridor lamp(s), entry/termination and thresholds. Preserve the central block/wing relationship in all establishing views. The story supplies order and adjacency, not dimensions. Maintain a source-labeled schematic; use no measured scale unless separately established.
- **Spoiler boundary:** no image, thumbnail, prompt, filename, alt text, shadow, animal, pipe, cord, vent, or other visual motif may disclose the eventual mechanism or solution before the story does. Do not add clues that are absent from an approved scene. Visuals may show only the evidence Watson is currently allowed to observe.

## Location continuity packet

Each approved location master must include: a labeled plan view; one canonical establishing angle; light-source arrows; door/window/furniture positions; material palette; known time/weather variants; crop-safe guide for `#visualStage`; and a list of scene facts that must not be introduced early. The creator approves geometry and visual treatment separately from individual scene art.
